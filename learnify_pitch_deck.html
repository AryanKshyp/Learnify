The problem

Students cannot see what is
missing from their own preparation.

They finish the problem lists, the playlists, the courses — and still fail. In the interview, a question surfaces a conceptual gap they never knew they had. You cannot fix a gap you cannot name, so the next round of preparation is more of the same, chosen by guesswork.

01 · INVISIBLE

You don't know what you don't know

Content completion is not knowledge. Finishing a playlist measures attendance, not understanding — and no tool distinguishes the two.

02 · UPSTREAM

The cause is four months back

Failing Naive Bayes is usually a Probability problem. Nothing on the market traces a failure to the prerequisite that produced it.

03 · DECAYING

Knowledge leaves quietly

What you studied in June is not what you know in November, and nothing tells you which parts went.

The pain is not that unprepared students fail. It's that prepared students fail — the ones who did the work and still could not see what was missing.

How you use it

One loop, run once per concept.

DAY ONE — THE TWELVE MINUTES

01

Pick your tracks

AI Engineer · SDE · Data · Quant, several at once. Three taps. This is the only setup step — no onboarding survey, no tutorial.

02

Start the session

We don't ask what you want to work on. The system picks from the diagnostic ladder — the concepts where surface familiarity and real understanding diverge most.

03

Get examined

It probes derivation depth, not recall. No hints, no encouragement. Hard stop at twelve minutes.

04

See the gap

Not a score — a sentence. "You can state Bayes' theorem. You couldn't derive it from the product rule. That's blocking four things on your track."

EVERY SESSION AFTER

The home screen only ever answers one question: what is tonight's hour worth most on? What's due for review, what's blocking the most downstream work, what has never been measured.

Every concept carries two numbers. What you proved — permanent, never altered by time. And what we can currently trust — proved × retention × prerequisite health. The student never sees either as a number. They see the consequence: "three things you knew in June need twenty minutes this week." And closing one upstream concept silently repairs everything beneath it.

Go-to-market

Five decisions that shape everything else.

01

The 2026 season is already gone. Don't chase it.

It's August; on-campus drives are starting and we have no voice. Trying to monetise this season is the most expensive mistake available. We use it as a research harvest instead.

02

Sell to the rejected, not the preparing.

Willingness to pay for diagnosis peaks in the 72 hours after a bad interview — that's when the invisible gap becomes visible. A student who thinks their prep is fine has no reason to buy a product that says otherwise.

03

Every channel must be earned. Paid acquisition is arithmetically dead.

Qualified signups cost ₹150–400 at ~5% conversion. That's ₹3,000–8,000 CAC against ₹1,600 ARPU. Campus, community, and referral-paid-in-voice-minutes are the only three that fit.

04

India builds the product. The diaspora funds it.

Indian-origin MS/PhD candidates abroad: same user, same channels, same COGS, ~3× the price. ~18% of base-case revenue for ~5% of the build.

05

Session length is the business model.

At 12 minutes, gross margin is ~70–75%. At 30 minutes it's ~40–55%. Shorter sessions are also better pedagogy and fresher evidence. Everything points the same direction.

Activation

Everything converts through twelve minutes.

One short voice session that surfaces one real gap the student did not know they had. Acquisition, word of mouth and conversion all run through that single moment.

The three things that will break it:

A

Opening concept selection

With no prior information, which concept does session one probe? Get it wrong and a strong student passes trivially and leaves. Fixed by the hand-curated diagnostic ladder from Phase 0.

B

Voice as a starting barrier

Nobody talks to a laptop at 11pm in a shared hostel room. Text mode is a first-class equal path — and it's also the free tier.

C

A generic gap statement

"Review probability" is worthless. The precision of the sentence is the activation event.

Teaching is the commodity we consume, not the product we sell. So metering teaching and giving away diagnosis isn't a compromise on the free tier — it's our positioning turned into a pricing rule.

One-time cost to generate the full explainer library at mid-tier quality: ~₹5,000. It removes ~40% of live tutoring turns permanently, and the content becomes reviewable, versionable and correctable — which text generated live and thrown away never is. Model routing follows the same logic: teach on mid-tier, probe on small, score on strong. A weak model teaching a derivation badly produces a student who confidently believes something wrong, and the engine records it as proved mastery.

THE DEPTH GATE — OUR BEST LIMIT

The curriculum already carries per-role depth targets. Free teaches to depth 2. Paid teaches to depth 3.

So the free tier can tell a student "you can state Bayes' theorem but you can't derive it" — and then cannot close it.

The founding story, converted into a paywall. It costs less to serve, it's honest about what it withholds, and the thing behind the wall is precisely the thing the student now knows they need.

THE ANCHOR

Scaler's flagship programmes sit around ₹3.25 lakh. Put that on the pricing page. Next to it, ₹2,499 stops being a price and becomes an obvious arbitrage.

WHY PACKS, NOT CREDITS

Per-session credits align price to cost perfectly — and make students ration assessments to save money. That corrupts the evidence, which is the one thing we cannot afford.

WHAT WE WILL NOT SELL

Not to employers as a hiring signal. Not content. No ads, no job board, no placement commission, no cross-student rankings — and no ISA. Each corrupts the incentive to be honest with the learner.

THE GUARDRAIL

No plan ships whose fully-consumed COGS exceeds 35% of its price. Cost per session, per user, per plan, on a dashboard from the first paying customer. Metered-backend startups don't die from bad pricing — they die from finding the p95 user twelve weeks late.

LEAKAGE TO BUDGET FOR — 10–15% ON TOP OF MODELLED COGS

·

Abandoned sessions. Mic fails at minute nine. We've paid for nine minutes, the student got nothing, we refund the credit and eat both.

·

Session overrun. A 12-minute session that drifts to 20 costs 65% more. Hard-stop in code, not in the prompt.

·

Refused scoring. Low-confidence transcripts we correctly decline to score still cost full price to produce.

·

Recording egress. ~12MB per session, append-only forever. Set retention before 100,000 sessions, not after.

THE OBJECTION EVERY TPO WILL RAISE

"Can I see a ranked list of my students?"

THE ANSWER, MEMORISED

No — and that refusal is why the data is worth anything. The moment a student thinks this reports on them, they perform instead of being honest, and you get the same useless number your current assessment gives you.

What you get instead is more actionable: 61% of this batch is blocked on the same four probability concepts, and your data track has 8% coverage against your SDE track's 40%. That tells you what to teach next week. A ranking tells you who to worry about, which you already know.

This converts a philosophical constraint into a positioning advantage and a privacy guarantee. It goes on the pricing page, not in a footnote.
